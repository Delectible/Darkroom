BTC–ETH pairs trading: does the edge survive costs?

I tested your 1% divergence idea and a properly validated z-score version, on real exchange candles, with costs, walk-forward selection and stress tests. Short answer: no out-of-sample edge, and nothing close to $150 a week.

Observed measured in historical data Assumption my modelling choice Optimised fitted on past data Out-of-sample data the choice never saw Projection forward-looking, speculative
VERDICT C · NO-GO

Historical evidence does not justify putting money into this bot.

The BTC/ETH relationship was not stable enough over the past year to trade intraday. After realistic costs, the 1% rule lost money on four of five timeframes. On the fifth (1 hour) it made +2.0% over 47 days but lost in the final, untouched stretch. The statistically selected version lost money out-of-sample on four of five timeframes. No result is statistically distinguishable from luck. Only 1 of 103 strategy-weeks reached $150.

One caveat runs through everything: the only market data reachable from this environment was Gemini spot candles. They cover 12 months only at daily resolution; intraday history is 14 to 91 days. That is enough to reject the strategy at the 3%-a-week level, but not enough to rule out a small multi-day edge. Section 12 says what would change my mind.

1 · Data

What was actually tested

Observed Source: Gemini exchange, spot BTC/USD and ETH/USD OHLCV candles from Gemini's public market-data API. Timestamps are UTC bar opens; close = last trade in the bar. All bars joined on timestamp, with no gaps on any timeframe, and hourly closes roll up exactly to daily closes. Binance, Bybit, OKX and every other exchange were blocked by this environment's network policy. Gemini returns about 1,460 bars per timeframe with no way to page further back, and offers no 4-hour candles, so 6-hour stands in for 4-hour.

Price convention: signals use closes. Every order fills at the next bar's open plus costs, so no trade uses a price it could not have had. P&L is tracked in USD returns on an account treated as A$5,000 Assumption: AUD/USD moves are ignored, but a USD-margined account carries that FX risk in practice. Gemini spot is thinner than the big perp venues (median 1-hour BTC volume about 3.5 BTC), so its candles are noisier than Binance's. I checked for bid–ask bounce artefacts: lag-1 autocorrelation of BTC returns is within ±0.03 on every timeframe.
2 · The relationship

BTC and ETH move together, but their price levels drift apart

Observed Daily returns correlate at 0.90, and ETH moves about 1.26× BTC on a typical day. Yet over the year ETH/BTC fell 11% and the full-year Engle–Granger cointegration test fails (p = 0.17). In rolling windows, cointegration passes at p < 0.05 in only 5–42% of windows depending on timeframe; with no relationship at all you'd expect 5%. The fitted hedge ratio wanders widely: 0.65 to 1.83 on 30-day windows, 0.2 to 1.7 on 1-week hourly windows. A static hedge ratio is therefore unsafe, and a rolling one is chasing a moving target.

How to read the variance ratio. VR(q) compares the spread's q-bar move to q × its one-bar move: below 1 means divergences tend to reverse; above 1 means they tend to keep going. Daily and 6-hour spreads mean-revert over roughly 4 days to a month (VR 0.4–0.6). The 15-minute and 30-minute spreads behave like a random walk (VR ≈ 1). The hourly VR above 1 is mostly one event, the 17–21 Aug 2026 ETH squeeze; without it the hourly VR is ≈1.0 out to 8 h and 0.66 at 32 h. Divergences that do revert take days, not hours. That works against a 1% intraday trigger. The in-window half-lives look short (1–25 bars), but they are biased low because the hedge ratio is fitted on the same window.
3 · Your original idea

The 1% rule, exactly as described

Assumption How I turned your words into rules: "established relationship" = rolling mean of log(ETH/BTC) over 60 days (daily), 14 days (6 h), 7 days (1 h), 4 days (30 m), 2 days (15 m). Enter when the ratio is ≥ 1% from that mean: short the rich coin, long the cheap one. Exit at 75% convergence (within 0.25%). Sizing is $2,500 each side (dollar-neutral, 1× gross), with no stop, no time limit and no filters. Nothing was fitted, so the whole period counts as out-of-sample for this rule. Trading starts after the longest lookback has warmed up, so the daily run covers 273 days (9 Jan – 9 Oct 2026) and the 1-hour run 47 days.

Observed On daily bars the rule loses 11.9% (base costs). ETH/BTC kept trending, so the average trade sat open for 32 days paying funding. On 1-hour bars it won 12 of 15 trades and made +2.0%. Most of that came early: in the last 30% of the period it lost 1.65%. The gross edge per trade (about 30 bps of notional) is only about twice the round-trip cost (about 14 bps), so doubling fees and slippage wipes it out. No threshold from 0.5% to 2% works across timeframes: the best one changes from timeframe to timeframe, and on daily bars all six lose.
4 · The statistical version

Z-score strategy with honest selection

Optimised Spread = residual of a rolling regression of log ETH on log BTC. z = residual ÷ its rolling standard deviation. Each timeframe's history was split into train (first 50%) → validation (next 20%) → untouched test (last 30%). A small pre-declared grid of 18 settings (3 lookbacks × entry |z| 1.5 / 2.0 / 2.5 × exit z 0 / 0.5) was scored on train. The top 3 went to validation, and the system stays flat if none is positive on both. Separately, a walk-forward re-selects from the same grid every week (intraday) or month (daily) using only the preceding window, again staying flat when nothing qualifies. Fixed defaults throughout: beta-neutral sizing, 1% equity at risk to a stop at entry + 1.5σ, max hold half the lookback, volatility-emergency exit, and the risk limits in section 7.

Out-of-sample Train → validation refused to trade on 1-day, 6-hour and 15-minute data, which is the "decide not to trade" behaviour working. Where it did pick settings (1 h, 30 m), the untouched test lost 3.0% and 3.3%. Walk-forward made +4.2% on 6-hour bars (8 trades over 6 weeks; t = 1.2, not significant) and lost on every other timeframe. Parameter stability: walk-forward chose 2–4 different setting combinations across 3–6 folds on every timeframe, so I flag the strategy as unstable.
5 · Overfitting check

Good in development, gone in the test

Every design variant in section 6 (hedge method, entry and exit rules, sizing, filters: 46–48 per timeframe) was run on the development period and on the untouched test. If design choices carried real information, dots would sit along the diagonal. Instead, most hourly and 30-minute variants are positive in development and negative in test. The rank correlation between dev and test is +0.19 (1 h), +0.33 (30 m), −0.72 (6 h) and +0.33 (1 d). That is the signature of selecting noise.

6 · Design questions

Static vs rolling hedge, % vs z, exits, sizing, filters

Each row changes one thing from the default (rolling hedge, mid lookback, enter |z| 2, exit z 0.5, beta sizing). Base costs. "Dev" is train + validation; "Test" is the untouched last 30%, shown for honesty but never used to choose.

What the tables say

  • Static vs rolling hedge. No consistent winner. Static looked fine on 30 m dev and lost on test; rolling lost in test everywhere. With a hedge ratio that drifts 0.2–1.9, neither is reliable.
  • % vs z-score thresholds. Z-scores are themselves volatility-adjusted thresholds. On test, z entries lost less than % entries on 1 d and 1 h, did about the same on 30 m, and did worse on 6 h, where 1.25–1.5% entries made +5–6% on test after losing in dev. No consistent improvement.
  • Exits and asymmetry. Wider entries (|z| ≥ 2.5) lost least on test at 1 h and 30 m; the exit level made no consistent difference. Every entry/exit combination still lost on the hourly test.
  • Sizing. Dollar-neutral had the smallest test loss on 1 h and 30 m; vol-scaled had the smallest on 1 d and the lowest daily dev drawdown; beta-neutral was never best on test. Beta-neutral is right in theory, but it leans on a hedge ratio that is badly estimated over short windows, and that estimation error costs more than the hedge saves. If anything is used, prefer dollar- or vol-neutral with a slow, return-based β check.
  • Filters. The cointegration filter cut trades and reduced test losses on every timeframe (1 h −2.9% → −2.0%, 30 m −3.1% → −2.6%, 6 h −5.0% → −2.1%), and gave the only positive daily test (+0.6%, 2 trades). On hourly dev it made things worse. The correlation filter never bound. The volatility-emergency exit fired rarely.

8 · The $150 question

One week in 103 made $150

Observed Weeks are 7-day blocks from each variant's start; the last block may be partial. Flat weeks are weeks with no trade. Across all ten variants, 1 of 103 strategy-weeks reached $150: +$168 in the week of 13 Mar 2026, from the daily 1% rule, which still lost 11.9% overall. Average weekly P&L was negative for 8 of 10 variants. The best (6-hour walk-forward) averaged +$35 a week over just 6 weeks.

9 · Costs, stress and Monte Carlo

Costs eat most of the gross edge

Assumption Both legs on USDT-margined perpetual futures (spot shorting needs margin borrow). No funding history was reachable, so funding is a flat drag on gross notional.

Per trade, round-trip costs came to about 14 bps of gross notional at base case. The 1% rule's hourly gross edge was about 30 bps per trade; the selected z-score strategies' out-of-sample gross edge was −6 bps (1 h), +4 bps (30 m) and +53 bps (6 h). Costs don't need to be large to destroy an edge that small.

Stress tests on three fixed reference strategies

A = your 1% rule (1 h). B = the 6-hour z-score settings walk-forward chose most often. C = the 1-hour settings walk-forward chose most often. B and C were picked with hindsight, so their baselines are in-sample and flattering; look at the changes, not the levels. Synthetic shocks are applied to the real price path, and multi-run rows show the mean, with the worst case alongside.

What breaks it: funding (5 bps per 8 h turns all three negative), execution delay on 6-hour bars (−4.4%), correlation dropping to 0.6 (5th-percentile runs −7% to −12%), and permanent divergence (worst runs −5 to −6%, with drawdowns near 10% despite the stops). A single crash bar (BTC −15%, ETH −22%) cost up to 8.5% even with the hedge on, because ETH falls further than the hedge assumes. The risk limits capped damage but did not create profit.

Monte Carlo: trade bootstrap, one year, by position size

Projection Resampling 9–15 historical trades only replays whatever edge those few trades happened to have. C's in-sample "+54%/yr" is not evidence; its own walk-forward lost money. The useful parts are the drawdown odds at each size, and the zero-edge rows: without a real edge, the coin-flip odds of a losing year stay near 50% before you even add costs. Even replaying C's flattering trades at 4× size gives only a 51% chance of averaging 3% a week, along with a 29% chance of a 30% drawdown.
10 · Risk controls used

Controls built for a $5,000 account

These were fixed in advance Assumption, never optimised. The system halts on drawdown and sits out the rest of a day or week after the loss limit. One position at a time. No entry when |z| is already past the stop, when short-term spread volatility is more than 2.5× normal, or (if enabled) when rolling cointegration p > 0.20.

11 · Other markets

Would the method suit other pairs?

Projection Not tested; no data was reachable. The method itself (rolling hedge, z-score, walk-forward selection, cost-first evaluation) carries over, but each market needs its own model:

  • AUD/USD vs NZD/USD: the most promising candidate on paper. The economies are closely linked, costs are a fraction of crypto's (sub-1 bp spreads), and there's no funding, only swap. But the spread moves maybe 0.2–0.5% a day, so returns need leverage, and central-bank divergence (RBA vs RBNZ) breaks the relationship for months at a time. Worth a separate test with 5+ years of hourly data.
  • Gold vs silver: the ratio trends for years, so mean reversion only appears over months. Futures roll costs and margin matter. A slow, low-turnover strategy at best; poor fit for a weekly target.
  • Other crypto pairs (e.g. SOL/ETH): higher idiosyncratic risk (unlocks, hacks, listings), thinner books, more expensive funding. Likely worse than BTC/ETH, not better.
12 · Verdict

C · NO-GO for live money

  • The 3% a week target is not supported. No variant came within a factor of four, and 1 of 103 strategy-weeks hit $150. Market-neutral BTC/ETH stat-arb that nets 0.1–0.5% a week in a good regime would already be a good result Projection.
  • Extra sophistication did not reliably help. On the same out-of-sample windows, the z-score version beat your 1% rule on 6-hour, 1-day and 15-minute bars but lost to it on 1-hour and 30-minute. Every comparison falls within the luck band: a pure coin-flip spread swings about ±9% over two months in this engine.
  • If you keep researching, look at multi-day horizons (6 h to daily bars, holds of days), the only place the data shows mean reversion, with a cointegration gate. Treat it as unproven.

What would change my mind

A re-run on 12+ months of Binance USDT-perp 15m/30m/1h/4h data with real funding history, using the same frozen protocol, where the walk-forward out-of-sample result clears all of these: ≥ 60 trades; t-stat on trade returns ≥ 2; positive at pessimistic costs; Sharpe ≥ 1; max drawdown ≤ 8%; the same parameter set chosen in ≥ 60% of folds. Then 8 weeks of paper trading, with live fills within 3 bps of the model. Unblock data.binance.vision in this environment's network settings and I can run that exact protocol.

13 · Re-test specification

The exact algorithm tested, for a like-for-like re-run

This is not a deployment spec. With a NO-GO verdict it's the algorithm a developer should reproduce on better data, paper-trade only, and kill if it fails the criteria above. Every rule below is what produced this report's numbers.

Data inputs
BTCUSDT and ETHUSDT perpetual klines (OHLCV, UTC), aligned on bar-open time; drop any bar missing either leg. Funding-rate history for both. Best bid/ask snapshot at each signal for the spread check.
Timeframe
Primary research candidate: 4 h (6 h here). Also re-test 1 h for comparison. Do not trade 15 m / 30 m; no evidence of reversion there.
Rolling windows
Lookback L ∈ {7, 14, 28} days (4 h/6 h) or {3, 7, 14} days (1 h). Vol-emergency short window = max(L/6, 4) bars. Correlation window = L/2.
Hedge ratio
Rolling OLS over the last L bars, including the bar just closed: ln ETH = α + β·ln BTC. β clipped to [0.2, 3.0] for sizing.
Spread
et = ln ETHt − αt − βt·ln BTCt (the regression residual).
Z-score
zt = et / σt, with σt = √(Var(ln ETH) − Cov²/Var(ln BTC)) over the same window: the residual standard deviation.
Cointegration test
Engle–Granger (ADF on the residual, MacKinnon p) over the last L bars, recomputed every L/12 bars. Gate: no new entry if p > 0.20; exit open position if p > 0.50.
Entry
|z| ≥ zin (grid 1.5 / 2.0 / 2.5) and |z| < zin + 1.5. z > 0: short ETH, long BTC. z < 0: long ETH, short BTC.
Exit
Converged: z crosses back to zout (grid 0 / 0.5) on the entry side. Or stop: |z| ≥ zin + 1.5. Or time: held ≥ L/2 bars. Or vol emergency, relationship break, or a risk limit.
Position sizing
Risk 1% of equity to the stop: NETH = 0.01·Equity / ((zstop − |z|)·σ + 2(1+β)·c). Gross G = NETH(1+β), capped at 2× equity. Legs: ETH = G/(1+β), BTC = G·β/(1+β).
Leverage limit
Gross notional ≤ 2.0× equity. Isolated margin at ≤ 3× per leg, so neither leg is near liquidation inside a 25% move.
Loss limits
Daily −2% (from UTC-day start equity): flatten, no entries until next UTC day. Weekly −4%: flatten until next week. Drawdown −15% from peak: flatten and halt until a human re-enables it.
Costs assumed
Per leg per fill: optimistic 2 bps, base 7 bps (5 taker + 0.5 half-spread + 1.5 slippage), pessimistic 12 bps. Funding drag on gross notional: 0 / 0.5 / 1.5 bps per 8 h; stress test at 5.
Execution
Signal on bar close. At next bar open, send both legs as marketable limit IOC orders at most 5 bps through the touch. If one leg fills and the other doesn't within 30 s, retry once, then flatten the filled leg at market and log a leg-failure. Never hold one leg alone past 60 s.
Parameter selection
Walk-forward: re-select from the 18-setting grid every test period (1 week on 1 h / 4 h) using the prior 3 weeks only. Score = return ÷ max(drawdown, 2%), with at least 3 trades. If the best score ≤ 0, stay flat for that period.
Monitoring and alerts
Push an alert on: any entry or exit; a leg-failure; position mismatch versus exchange (reconcile every minute); API errors 3 times in 5 minutes (stop new entries); stale data (no candle within 2 minutes of bar close); a funding rate above 3 bps per 8 h on the paying leg; any loss limit or halt; the cointegration gate closing. The twice-daily check reviews equity, open position and alert log.
