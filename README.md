# Darkroom

A camera app that makes you wait for your photos.

Most camera apps give you the picture instantly and a hundred filters to pick from. Darkroom does the opposite: you pick an old film or an old camera, take the shot, and live with how it turns out. Film has to develop. Digital shots sit on a memory card until you copy them off. It's meant to make taking pictures feel a bit more like it used to.

## Film Mode

- **Four films:** Ektar 100 (punchy colour), Portra 400 (warm, soft), HP5 Plus 400 (black & white) and a Super 8 movie camera.
- Every shot goes to the **darkroom** and takes about 5 minutes to develop. You get a quiet notification when it's ready.
- Developed prints get pinned to a **corkboard**. Super 8 reels play back on a projector.
- Nothing goes to your phone's gallery until you hit **Save** (or **Save all**).
- Each film has its own colour and grain. You can make the grain weaker or stronger in settings.

## Digital Mode

- **Four cameras:** a 1999 floppy-disk camera, a 2003 CCD compact, a Y2K flip phone and a 90s camcorder (video with sound).
- Shots are saved to a pretend **SD card**, browsed in a Windows 98–style file explorer.
- Files on the card are locked until you move them to the **C: drive**, which also copies them to your gallery.
- There are a few hidden things in the explorer's menus.

## Getting it

Android only for now (the iPhone version hasn't been tested yet).

1. Open **Releases** on this page and download the latest `app-release.apk` on your phone.
2. Open it and allow your browser to install apps when Android asks.

A new build appears in Releases automatically whenever the code is updated.
